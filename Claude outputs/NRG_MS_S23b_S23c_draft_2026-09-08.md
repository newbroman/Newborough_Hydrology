## S.23b Script 43 — Ranwell (1959) water-table sites: hand placement over a georeferenced sketch, height-checked and basin-tested

**Step [43 of N], Phase 17 in *run_analysis.py*. Display/utility tier (*tier="D"*, *exec="default"*). Added 2026-09-06 (D-140); rebuilt 2026-09-08 as v2.0.0 when D-140's Revisit-if was met. Skips cleanly when its placement input is absent. Script 44 reads its basins and positions.**

**Motivation.** Ranwell (1959) instrumented Newborough Warren with seventeen water-table pipes half a century before the present network, levelled each to Ordnance Datum, and read them fortnightly from 1951 to 1953. Where those pipes stood is the question that turns his readings (§S.23c) from an anecdote into a comparison. His Fig. 3 gives the sites as numbered points on a sketch with a table of OD heights. The first issue of this step (D-140) registered that sketch by a similarity transform anchored on Penlon Lake, with rotation assumed grid-north-up. On 2026-09-07 the author overlaid the sketch on a georeferenced base and found it non-metric: a field sketch, topologically right — which slack, what lies beside what — but not rigidly or affinely registrable. The similarity placements were tens to hundreds of metres from the sites' slacks (*ranwell_routeA_rms_error_m*), the assumed rotation being the largest part. v2 replaces registration with placement, and tests the placement in ways that do not depend on the sketch being metric.

**Inputs.** Read via *utils.paths*. The headline positions are *ranwell_1959_sites_martin.csv*: the author's hand placement of all seventeen sites in Google Earth over his own warp of the sketch, with the slack each site is drawn in (*sketch_slack*: PL Penlon, CG Clwt Gwlyb, and the two unnamed slacks holding sites 3–10 and 18, and 2, 5, 6, 14 and 15) and the tabulated height. The warp and the scan are in copyright and are in neither repository (the D-081 shape); *ranwell_1959_sites_georef_px.csv* carries the numeral positions read off the warp, so the hand move from them is visible in the output. The author's traced slack outlines and ridge (*ranwell_features.kml*, *ranwell_ridge.kml*) are drawn on the overlay for comparison only. The DEM, the modern network (*01_locations.csv* with the reference and extended lists) and the retired first-issue inputs complete the set; provenance is in *GEO_PROVENANCE.md*. Constants are the *RANWELL_* block of *config.py*.

**Methodology — four routes, in order of authority.** Route M is the placement itself. Route H is the height check: Ranwell's levelling is set against the DEM (sampled bilinearly) at each placed site; a single datum offset is fitted as the median residual over the sites on gentle ground (slope below *RANWELL_GENTLE_SLOPE* on a DEM smoothed at *RANWELL_SLOPE_SMOOTH_M*), and each site is then moved within *RANWELL_H_SEARCH_M* to the cell minimising the sum of the squared height residual over *RANWELL_H_SIGMA_M* and the squared move over *RANWELL_D_SIGMA_M*. Every site carries a *resolvability*: *flank* where the ground slopes and the height fixes position, *flat_floor* where many cells within the search radius match the height (more than *RANWELL_FLAT_CELLS* within *RANWELL_H_ACCEPT_M*) and the height cannot resolve position, *sparse_match* otherwise. Route T is a topological test: the DEM, smoothed at *RANWELL_DEM_SMOOTH_M*, is segmented into closed basins by a watershed seeded from minima at least *RANWELL_BASIN_HMIN_M* deep, and each site's basin is compared with the basins of the other sites the sketch draws in the same slack; a site is *basin_consistent* when it shares a basin with one of them or sits in an adjacent one. Ridge crests are the basin divides. Route F floods each slack group from the sites' local floors (the *RANWELL_FLOOR_PCT* percentile of the DEM within *RANWELL_FLOOR_WINDOW_M*) by a documented height *RANWELL_SLACK_DELTA_M*, clipped to the group's basins and to *RANWELL_SLACK_REACH_M* of the sites, and records per group whether the floor is bounded by the ground or by the reach. Route A, the retired similarity registration, is run and written as a diagnostic. Each site is matched to its nearest modern dipwells, with a same-basin flag, and every modern well's basin is written for Script 44.

**Site-specific choices and rationale (D-140, amended 2026-09-08).** The sketch's slack outlines are not tested, only its grouping, because the 2026-09-08 sweep over *RANWELL_SLACK_DELTA_M* showed that they resolve on the DEM only where a slack is a closed trough (the coastal slack of sites 3–10 and 18): the Penlon, Clwt Gwlyb and 2–15 slacks are sections of continuous low ground along the dune-slack corridors, bounded at every trial height by the search reach rather than the ground, and sites 12 and 13, drawn in a discrete slack, lie in one corridor. The outline is therefore no more a metric object than the site position, and the basin test is what the DEM can actually pass or fail. The per-group heights in *RANWELL_SLACK_DELTA_M* are those at which every site of the group stands on its floor; they are documented constants, not fitted ones. The datum offset is fitted on gentle ground because a flank site's residual measures its position, not the datum.

**Outputs.** *outputs/43_ranwell/* carries *43_01_ranwell_sites.csv* (per site, every route side by side: the placement, the numeral position and hand move, the height, the DEM height and residual before and after the offset, the refined position, move and residual, slope, matching cells and resolvability, basin and the three basin flags, distance to the group's floor, the Route A position and its error, the nearest well and whether it shares the basin), *43_02_nearest_well.csv*, *43_03_registration_diagnostic.csv* (the offset and its MAD, the gentle-site count, the cost constants, the counts within 0.3 m, on flanks and basin-consistent, the basin count and exceptions, the Route A rms), *43_03b_slack_floors.csv* (per group: delta, floor, area, *bounded_by*, sites on the floor), *43_04_overlay.png* (hillshade, basin divides, floors, the traced outlines, every route's points), *43_05_ranwell_sites.geojson*, *43_06_slack_floors.geojson* (floors and divides), *43_07_modern_well_basins.csv* and *43_report_numbers.csv* (*ranwell_sites_placed*, *ranwell_datum_offset_m*, *ranwell_sites_within_0p3m*, *ranwell_sites_flank*, *ranwell_sites_basin_consistent*, *ranwell_routeA_rms_error_m*, *ranwell_modern_wells_with_analogue*). In the committed run the datum offset is of order a decimetre with a MAD of the same order (his benchmark agrees with the modern DEM to within levelling and placement error), most sites sit within 0.3 m of the DEM before any refinement, refinement moves are metres except at the two sites the heights move onto a flank, and every site is basin-consistent.

**Limitations and known caveats.** The positions are a placement corroborated by heights and basins, not a survey: on a flat slack floor the height cannot resolve position and *RANWELL_POS_SIGMA_M* is the honest statement of that, which Script 44 carries as an error term. The datum offset's MAD, not its value, is the datum uncertainty downstream. The basin test is against the sketch's grouping, which is the one thing a field sketch does record reliably; it cannot detect a site placed in the right basin but the wrong part of it. Route F is a diagnostic layer and nothing reads it. The pipes' construction and depth are known only from Ranwell's text (about 95 cm), and readings deeper than that exist, so the depth was not a hard limit.

**Where it appears.** Through Script 44 (§S.23c) and its report paragraph; the placement itself is not quoted in the report. The provenance ruling is D-140, amended 2026-09-08.

## S.23c Script 44 — Ranwell's 1951–53 readings against the SSM hindcast and the modern water-table surface

**Step [44 of N], Phase 17 in *run_analysis.py*. Analytical tier (*tier="A"*, *exec="default"*). Added 2026-09-08; D-145 (W140). Reads three raw inputs no pipeline step produces — the digitised Ranwell tables, the exception D-145 records as D-051 did for Script 39 — and skips cleanly when they are absent. Reads Script 43's basins and positions.**

**Motivation.** Every test of the SSM lies inside or near its calibration window except Script 39's, which reaches back to 1989. Ranwell's readings reach back to 1951, to the start of the useful climate record and to a warren the forest had only begun to cover. They allow two things the corpus could not do before: an out-of-sample test of the fitted coefficients fifty-four years before calibration, and a measured answer to the question the secondary literature has asserted for decades without a measurement — whether the slack water table has fallen since the 1950s. Ranwell himself claimed no decline; what he printed is a seasonal range and a baseline, and the report's historical paragraph is rewritten from this step's output rather than from the literature that cites him.

**Inputs.** Ranwell (1959) printed no table. Three files in *data/* recover what he printed: *ranwell_1951_53_water_levels.csv*, the Fig. 4 series (Sites 1 and 4 for 1951–53, Site 8 for 1951) as dated depths and OD levels; *ranwell_1951_53_monthly_ranges.csv*, the Fig. 7 monthly ranges for seven sites (1, 4, 11, 12, 13, 16, 18), all years pooled as the figure pools them; and *ranwell_1950_53_parc_mawr_rain.csv*, the Fig. 2 monthly rainfall at the Parc Mawr nursery. Their digitisation, calibration, accuracy and the independent cross-check between the two water-table figures are in *data/RANWELL_PROVENANCE.md*. No BS-slack site (2, 5, 6, 14, 15) has a printed series. From the pipeline: *43_01_ranwell_sites.csv*, *43_07_modern_well_basins.csv* and *43_03_registration_diagnostic.csv* (Script 43); *01_climate.csv*, *03_master_data.csv*, *01_wells_clean.csv*, *01_locations.csv*; the 2006 coastline and Script 40's epoch series for context. Constants are the Script 44 block of *RANWELL_* in *config.py*, and the β₁ scalings of Script 39.

**Methodology — three questions, in decreasing order of what the data bear.** *The hindcast.* Each site with a printed series is paired with every modern well that shares its DEM basin and lies within *RANWELL_PAIR_MAX_M*. Each paired well's committed Model A coefficients are driven by RAF Valley climate from the start of the record through *utils.model_utils.simulate_ssm*, exactly as Script 39, and the modelled mid-month level (the mean of consecutive end-of-month levels, to match instantaneous fortnightly readings) is set against the monthly mean of Ranwell's readings after removing the mean offset — the two are not at the same point and a metre of ground separates them — and after capping the model at the ground surface, because a flooded slack reads at the surface and the observation is censored there (the offset is fitted on the months at least *RANWELL_SURFACE_CENSOR_M* below ground). The metrics are r, the Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency after the offset, the seasonal ranges and the month of the 1951 minimum, at the base coefficients and across Script 39's β₁-scaling envelope; the nearest paired well is the headline and every pairing is written, so the spread among wells in one slack is visible. The forcing is checked over the same months: Parc Mawr against RAF Valley, as a ratio of totals and a monthly correlation. *The level.* Ranwell's mean level at each printed site (the reading mean where the series exists, else the mean of the monthly mid-ranges; the two agree to *RANWELL_SAMPLING_SIGMA_M* where both exist) is set against the modern mean water-table surface interpolated to the site by inverse-distance weighting of per-well modern means (*RANWELL_IDW_K* wells within *RANWELL_IDW_RADIUS_M*, power *RANWELL_IDW_POWER*, wells with at least *RANWELL_MIN_MODERN_N* readings), at the placed and at the height-refined position, and the climate-only expectation for the two spans — the hindcast's own 1951–53 mean less its modern-span mean at the headline well — is subtracted. Four error terms are combined in quadrature per site: the leave-one-out root-mean-square error of the interpolation over the contributing wells; the surface gradient (central differences over *RANWELL_GRAD_STEP_M*) times the positional uncertainty from Route H (*RANWELL_POS_SIGMA_M* on a flat floor, the refinement move on a flank); the sampling difference between a reading mean and a mid-range mean; and the datum offset's MAD from Route H. Sites combine by inverse-variance weighting with the chi-square per degree of freedom reported, once over every site and once over the sites whose modern surface is constrained (leave-one-out error at or below *RANWELL_LOO_MAX_M*), a property of the network rather than of any site by name. *The rate.* The combined and per-site changes over the interval between the record midpoints, and whether each is resolved at two standard errors.

**Site-specific choices and rationale (D-145).** The comparison is made at the basin, not the point: the 2026-08-22 session abandoned per-site comparison because positional error exceeded the signal, and Script 43 v2 removed most of that objection while leaving the remainder as a term the error model states rather than a reason not to compare. The offset-removed hindcast is the one test that does not depend on position beyond "same slack". The cap at the surface is the same instrument-matching step the seasonal-range comparison of 2026-08-22 applied to the modern network. The forcing check exists because the hindcast years are the earliest the RAF Valley record is asked to carry; Ranwell's own gauge is the only independent test of it. Site 8, at the seaward end of the coastal slack, is the one site more than a metre below Ranwell's level; its modern surface rests on one well and is unconstrained by the leave-one-out criterion, and Ranwell recorded that end of the slack as accreting, so it is reported and not explained.

**Outputs.** *outputs/44_ranwell_hindcast/* carries *44_01_ranwell_readings.csv* and *44_02_ranwell_monthly_ranges.csv* (the inputs joined to site, basin and headline well), *44_03_hindcast_series.csv* (monthly observed mean and modelled level per site and paired well), *44_04_hindcast_metrics.csv* (per pairing: n and months at the surface, offset, r, NSE after offset, ranges, timing, the climate-only expectation and the β₁ envelope), *44_05_level_change.csv* (per site: the Ranwell mean and its basis, the modern surface at both positions, the contributing wells, the four error terms and their sum, the climate expectation and its source, Δ, z, the interval and rate, *resolved* and *surface_constrained*; and the two *COMBINED* rows with their standard error and chi-square), *44_06_climate_check.csv*, *44_07_hindcast.png*, *44_08_level_change.png* and *44_report_numbers.csv* (*ranwell_hindcast_r_median* and *_r_min*, *ranwell_hindcast_nse_median*, *ranwell_sites_compared*, *ranwell_sites_surface_constrained*, *ranwell_interval_years*, *ranwell_forcing_ratio_1951_53*, *ranwell_forcing_r_1951_53*, the per-site *ranwell_delta_m_siteN* and *ranwell_sigma_m_siteN*, and for each combination *ranwell_delta_m_…*, *ranwell_sigma_m_…*, *ranwell_chi2_dof_…*, *ranwell_rate_mm_yr_…*, *ranwell_resolved_…*). In the committed run the headline pairings reproduce the shape and, once capped, the amplitude of the 1951–53 series (r and NSE after offset by key); the forcing ratio is close to unity; the combined change since 1951–53 is of order a decimetre downward with a standard error of the same order and is not resolved at two standard errors, nor is any single site; the chi-square per degree of freedom is modestly above one.

**Limitations and known caveats.** Coefficient stationarity is assumed, as in Script 39, and the β₁ envelope is the statement of it. The readings are recovered from printed figures to about a centimetre and are a full-period sample of one wet and two dry years, not a long-term mean; the hindcast's climate expectation corrects for that to the extent the model is right. The modern surface within a slack is not flat, which is why the interpolation error dominates the budget and why two coastal-slack sites are unconstrained. Nothing is said about the BS slack or about the ground under the canopy, where Ranwell had no pipe; the forest drawdown the report models at the Clwt Gwlyb sites' distances from the canopy is smaller than this comparison's error and is neither confirmed nor excluded by it. A rate over the interval is a bound, not a measurement, until a site resolves.

**Where it appears.** Report §1 (the historical paragraph, rewritten from *44_05*) and the validation discussion beside Script 39; Paper 1 by one sentence in the out-of-sample paragraph, under D-144's reason requirement. The provenance rulings are D-145 and D-140.
