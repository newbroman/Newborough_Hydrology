# Drumlin hypothesis for the C2/C3 divide — research memo

**Status:** working finding / hypothesis assessment. NOT a manuscript claim. Prepared 2026-09-19 at Martin's request ("might drumlins, visible at the coast, be responsible for the C2/C3 divide?"). Verdict below is a plausibility assessment for the discussion, to be weighed by Martin.

## Bottom line
**PLAUSIBLE, but currently UNSUPPORTED as a *distinct* mechanism — and largely redundant with what the report already says. Not contradicted.** A buried till high (drumlin) is a physically valid way to produce the C2/C3 contrast, but it is essentially a specific named cause for the "shallower sand over till (east) vs deep sand (west)" block contrast the report already invokes. The well geometry weakly disfavours a single ice-flow-parallel drumlin ridge (the divide runs coast-parallel, ~N-S, ~30-45 degrees off the expected NE-SW drumlin grain) but cannot exclude a drumlinised till surface. The single decisive check is to map the till-surface (aquifer-base) elevation across the boundary (borehole logs + a short coring transect), cross-checked against a LiDAR lineament scan and BGS superficial mapping.

## How the report currently explains the C2/C3 divide
Already framed as a till-base-geometry contrast (Stratford et al., 2007):
- Site setting (report7): the dune field is underlain by weakly permeable glacial till forming the effective base of the sand aquifer; a bedrock ridge trends SW-NE.
- The divide (report7): eastern block = shallower sand over till and estuarine deposits (responsive, heterogeneous); western block = deep sand over a broader platform (high storage, buffered).
- Clustering (report9 s4.2): primary split separates till-and-estuary-influenced eastern aquifer (C1, C2) from the deeper, more uniform western sand block (C3+).
- Head offset: C3 runs ~0.3 m deeper than C2 (mean -0.89 vs -0.60 m), attributed to deeper sand accumulation and higher storage.
- Supporting storage/drainage contrast: Sy C1 0.21 -> C2 0.269 -> C3 0.328; drainage half-life C2 ~9.9 mo vs C3 ~13.6 mo ("shallow pan" vs "deep sponge").
- Repeated caveat: the till-base/bedrock geometry is unconstrained without coring.

So the report's model already IS a till-base-topography model; it simply does not name a *drumlin* as the reason the eastern till is shallow.

## What the literature documents (Anglesey / Newborough drumlins)
- Anglesey was overridden by a SW-flowing Irish Sea ice stream, leaving a swarm of drumlins and thick glacial (Irish Sea) till; Newborough/Malltraeth sands are reworked from these deposits. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Anglesey
- Hart, J.K. (1995), "Drumlin formation in Southern Anglesey and Arvon, Northwest Wales," J. Quaternary Science 10(1):3-14 — drumlins documented in southern Anglesey (includes Newborough/Malltraeth district). https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jqs.3390100103
- "Bedrock controls on subglacial landform distribution ... Late Devensian Irish Sea Ice Stream" (Geomorphology, 2009) — maps Anglesey glacigenic landforms incl. drumlins under the ISIS. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0037073809002619
- Greenly, E. (1919), The Geology of Anglesey (BGS memoir) — drumlins and boulder-clays by district. https://geoguide.scottishgeologytrust.org/p/geologyofanglesey/bgs_anglesey_000_contents
- Mapped Anglesey drumlin RIGS sites exist (e.g. Penrhos). https://www.geomon.co.uk/penrhos-drumlin-rigs-site/

Note: no published evidence found of a *specific* drumlin beneath Newborough Warren itself; what is documented is (i) drumlins in southern Anglesey generally and (ii) a till substrate at the site (also in the report). Ice flowed SW, so drumlin long axes should trend ~NE-SW (parallel to flow), the same trend as the site's bedrock ridge.

## Mechanistic assessment
A drumlin is a streamlined till hill. A till high beneath the eastern dune would give a locally shallow impermeable base -> thin saturated sand -> low storage, flashy response, fast drainage — the C2/eastern-block signature — with sand thickening off its flank into C3. So the mechanism maps cleanly onto the report's shallow-till-east / deep-sand-west contrast: plausible. Qualifications:
1. Not a competing hypothesis so much as a specific case of the existing one; confirming a drumlin would refine, not overturn, the model.
2. The report links the eastern shallow-till zone to the estuarine/tidal margin, not to a drumlin — an estuarine/lacustrine till-and-clay origin is a different story from a subglacial drumlin, even if both leave a shallow low-permeability base.
3. "Visible at the coast" cuts against the geometry: the eroding, substrate-exposing coast is the *western* Caernarfon Bay foreshore, which is the C3 (deep-sand) side. Wave-form till exposed there would imply shallow till in the *west*, opposite to the model. If the forms are on the southern/Menai (Llanddwyn) side, that is consistent with the eastern till block. The hypothesis needs to specify which coast the wave-forms are seen at, and on which side of the divide.

## Well geometry (from outputs/01_locations.csv + outputs/02_cluster_stats.csv)
- C2 (Dune, n=24): mean E 242,559, ~1,508 m from coast (eastern, inland).
- C3 (Western Residual, n=21): mean E 241,669, ~788 m from coast (western, more coastal).
- Separation vector C3->C2 ~078 degrees (nearly due east); implied divide trends ~N-S. Eastings OVERLAP — a gradational, hydrograph-defined boundary (mostly "Fuzzy" wells), not a clean spatial cut.
- A single ice-flow-parallel drumlin ridge would trend ~NE-SW (~045 degrees); the observed divide (~N-S) is ~30-45 degrees oblique and instead roughly parallel to the W-facing coast — a better match to a shore-normal storage wedge (sand thickening seaward into C3) than to one drumlin. A drumlin *field* (a swarm of NE-SW ridges) could still impose an E-W mean change in till-base depth, and ~45 sparse gradational wells cannot resolve individual ridges.
- Net: geometry weakly disfavours a single-drumlin control but cannot exclude a drumlinised till surface.

## What would settle it (cheapest -> definitive)
1. LiDAR lineament/hillshade analysis of the 2 m DEM already held — look for NE-SW streamlined ridges/swales in the till surface (free, immediate).
2. BGS 1:50k superficial-geology sheet for SW Anglesey — does it map till (vs blown sand) or a drumlin under the warren, and where is the till/sand contact.
3. Existing borehole/dipwell logs — map depth-to-till (sand thickness) across the C2/C3 boundary (coverage is the limiting factor; largely absent).
4. Definitive: a short coring transect across the C2/C3 boundary to map the till-surface elevation.

If the till surface rises into a NE-SW-elongated high under C2 and drops away westward under C3, the drumlin reading is confirmed; if the base deepens smoothly seaward with no streamlined form, the coast-parallel sand-wedge explanation holds and the drumlin hypothesis is refuted.
