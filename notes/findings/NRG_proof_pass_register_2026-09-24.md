### On Martin's "4.10.3 reads largely as discussion"

Taken at the section the map numbers 4.10.3 (far-field level): three of its five paragraphs are quantitative (VIF, rolling-window statistics, PET and rainfall trends); the other two (L894–896) are exactly the register the rubric flags — an argument about what a near-zero far-field constant does and does not mean ("A constant near zero invites the reading that the climate is not changing. It is not that." … "it is evidence that the change here is seasonal in character"). So: mixed, with the argument concentrated. The important finding is the concordance: **§5 carries this argument only as a passing mention (5.7.7, L424)**, so moving it is a transfer, not a deletion — the reasoning would be lost if simply cut. Taken at 4.10.2 (network-scale partition), the section is mostly fit statistics; its one real interpretive claim is L870, "moves from a qualitative candidate to the dominant quantified contributor to the C5 anomaly", which ranks coastal retreat against two named competitors and has no §5 home either (5.7.2 corroborates the C5 anomaly but does not carry this ranking).

**§4.12 is the stronger case than either.** It duplicates §5.7.5 near word-for-word in at least four places (see 3A).
