Reading against the three candidate mechanisms:

  (1) CANOPY-INTERCEPTION OVER-ESTIMATION — not supported as the
      primary driver. The most significant winter contrast sits in
      an OPEN-DUNE cluster (C3, away from canopy), while C5 (Coastal
      Forest — forested but ridge-distant) shows NO winter peak.
      Interception over-estimation predicts the opposite (both
      forest clusters peak, open dune does not). Evidence runs
      against it; no data-driven case to revise F = 0.24.

  (2) WINTER-PHASED NONLINEAR RECHARGE (site-wide) — supported.
      Open-dune C3 carries a robust, highly significant winter
      contrast (p < 0.001), with C1 / C2 / C4 positive in sign
      (individually non-significant).

  (3) RIDGE-DERIVED LATERAL INPUT — partially consistent, not
      separable from (2) here. Within the forest
      (n = 14 wells), the contrast correlates with distance-
      to-ridge at r = -0.55 — i.e. it strengthens toward
      the ridge, the direction ridge subsidy predicts. The small
      forest sample makes this suggestive only, and the strongest
      peak overall is open-dune C3 (several of whose wells also lie
      near the ridge), so a ridge term and a site-wide recharge term
      cannot be distinguished on this evidence.
      (Contrast vs forest-edge distance: r = +0.28.)

Preferred reading:
  The winter contrast is NOT forest-confined, so canopy-interception
  over-estimation is not the primary driver — no evidence-based case
  here to revise F = 0.24. The signal is best explained by a broadly
  site-wide winter recharge non-linearity, possibly with a ridge-
  derived component in the forest; the two are not separable on the
  cluster-stratified climatology alone. A fuller attribution of the
  residual seasonality to specific model terms is left to future work.

All contrasts: point estimate, well-level bootstrap 95% CI
(1000 resamples), two-sided one-sample t p-value.
No multiple-comparison correction (descriptive across five clusters).
